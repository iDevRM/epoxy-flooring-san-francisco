What Does Commercial Epoxy Flooring Involve?

Commercial work starts with a site visit to look at the slab, the drains, the equipment and your schedule. We plan the job in sections or after hours so you can keep operating.

The floor is ground or shot-blasted to bare concrete, damaged areas are rebuilt and the slab is sloped toward drains where needed. Then the system goes down: epoxy for dry areas, or a heavier urethane cement system for kitchens and breweries that see hot water and thermal shock.

Where the floor meets the wall, we form a coved base so there's no seam for water and food debris to hide in.

  • Water is getting under the old flooring near drains

Why Do Commercial Floors Break Down?

Commercial floors fail from a mix of water, chemicals, heat and traffic. Hot-water washdowns and cold spills cause the concrete and coating to expand and contract quickly, which cracks thin coatings and loosens tile.

Grease, sugar, cleaning chemicals and brewery sanitizers eat into bare concrete and grout. Carts, dollies and pallet jacks grind away at weak spots until they become holes.

The fix is a system rated for the actual conditions, installed over properly prepared concrete, with sealed edges at walls and drains.

Epoxy vs. Urethane Cement: Which Do You Need?

Both are seamless resin floors. The right choice depends on heat, water and chemicals in the space.

EpoxyUrethane cement
Best forRetail, offices, stockrooms, dry areasKitchens, breweries, washdown areas
Hot water and thermal shockLimitedBuilt for it
FinishSmooth gloss or flakeTextured, anti-slip
Moisture toleranceNeeds a dry slab or primerTolerates damper slabs

Many restaurants use urethane cement in the kitchen and dish area and epoxy in dry storage or the dining room. We'll map that out with you at the estimate.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do epoxy floors meet health code for restaurants?

California's food code calls for floors in food prep areas that are smooth, durable, nonabsorbent and easy to clean, with a sealed base where floor meets wall. Properly installed resin floors with a coved base are commonly used to meet those requirements, and we can work with your plans for inspection.

Can you install a commercial floor without closing my business?

In most cases, yes. We work nights, weekends or in sections, and fast-curing systems can let a floor reopen the next morning. We plan the schedule with you before the job starts.

How long does a commercial epoxy floor last?

A properly installed commercial system usually lasts many years, depending on traffic and cleaning. Heavy-use kitchens and breweries last longest with urethane cement, and worn areas can often be recoated instead of fully replaced.

Can you coat around kitchen equipment and drains?

Yes. Movable equipment is pulled out, and fixed equipment, drains and curbs are detailed so the floor seals tight around them. Drains are protected during work and the floor is sloped toward them where needed.
